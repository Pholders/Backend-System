PHolders Phase 1 Testing

Phase 1 is one loop that crosses four apps. Every story below is checked off by a person, with their name and any notes kept against it, so the team can see what has actually been exercised rather than what was built.

The backend is deployed and the loop works end to end. Proven on the live system: a doctor signs a script, the patient sends it to a pharmacy, the pharmacy dispenses it, and the doctor sees it come back. Also verified — patient doctor search, which used to fail for every signed-in patient; the medical aid cover filter, including a scheme typed differently to the doctor's; the QR code's one-time lifecycle; pharmacy activation; and medical aid encryption.

One known gap. Prescription QR links still carry http://localhost:3000, because the web app that serves them is not deployed yet. The QR itself works — a pharmacy scanning the code is unaffected — but the emailed link will not open. The stories in The prescription QR link are expected to fail until that app ships.

New on 9 October. Downloading a prescription now produces an actual PDF instead of JSON describing one, and claims and invoices accept the supporting document their column was always meant to hold. File storage moved behind a driver so it can point at Firebase instead of a disk that does not survive a deploy. None of this has been exercised by anyone yet, so Documents and PDFs is the newest and least trustworthy section on this page.

Least tested: the pharmacy screens. The queue, claiming and dispensing are all confirmed working through the API, but nobody has clicked through the screens themselves, so that section is the most likely to turn up something real.

Known, not defects

  • SMS and push reminders are console stubs.
  • Uploaded files are erased by the next deploy until STORAGE_DRIVER is set to firebase on Render. The Firebase project has not been created yet.
  • Nothing in the platform creates invoice rows, so an invoice has no document to attach to. Claims are created by the pharmacy order flow and do work.
  • A prescription PDF is generated on each request and never stored, so there is nothing to go stale.
  • Prescription QR links point at localhost until the web app is deployed.
  • Inventory has no backend at all — no routes, controller or model.
  • Refills do not exist as a concept anywhere.
  • Sick notes have no API. The requirement exists (FR-G-020) but is not in phase 1.
  • Doctor billing is out of scope in the SRS, twice.
  • Patient records (PHR) are phase 3.
  • Editing a signed prescription is forbidden by BR-123, by design.
  • Dispensing is not offered in the doctor app; pharmacies dispense, doctors display.
